Verdict & the five red lines

Legal as described — viewers pay nothing, creators pay for advertising. No stakes means no gambling, no "online money game" under the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025 (in force 1 May 2026), and no 28%-on-stakes GST. But each red line below is a trap door: one paid-retry button turns the whole product into a banned money game (up to 3 years / ₹1 crore).

RED LINE 01Viewers never pay or stakeNo entry fees, paid retries, coins/boosts, deposit-to-withdraw — ever. "Other stakes" includes anything bought with money.
RED LINE 02No earnings from recruitingNo referral chains, no % of downline activity. Otherwise: Prize Chits & Money Circulation Act (Ponzi law).
RED LINE 03Every paid slot labelled"Sponsored / Paid placement" visible on the card. Hidden paid ranking = misleading ad, CCPA fines ₹10–50 lakh.
RED LINE 04Honour published reward rulesTransparent "real time spent" measurement, payout timelines, logged and appealable denials. Opaque denials = unfair trade practice.
RED LINE 0518+ only, KYC at payoutDPDP Act bans behavioural tracking of under-18s — your attention/anti-fraud tracking is exactly that. Age-gate at signup, PAN at payout.

Reward model compared: nothing · coupons · cash

Compliance scales with what you give viewers. No rewards = an ordinary sponsored-listings site (most duties vanish). Coupons = same gaming/prize/TDS rules as cash, but simpler GST and no bank rails. Cash = the full stack.

Coupon rules of thumb: use third-party brand vouchers only (never issue your own stored-value balance — RBI PPI territory); keep coupons unpurchasable, non-tradeable, never cash-outable; disclose validity, brand and min-spend at the moment of the offer (CCPA); single-use codes against resale.     Quick law map

    QuestionAnswer
    Is watch-to-earn gambling?No — as long as visitors never pay or stake anything to participate. No stake ⇒ no betting, no money game.
    Does the 2025 Gaming Act ban it?No — it bans games where the user pays fees/deposits/stakes expecting winnings, skill or chance irrelevant. Viewers pay nothing ⇒ permitted no-stakes territory. Criminal trap if you ever charge viewers.
    Is pay-to-rank legal?Yes — paid placement is advertising. But bought slots must be visibly disclosed; hiding them is a misleading advertisement (fines ₹10–50 lakh).
    Tax on viewer rewards?30% TDS (Sec. 194B) once a user's winnings cross ₹10,000/financial year; flat 30% tax for the user. Creator fees: 18% GST.
    Can we skip GST?Yes, legally — below ₹20 lakh/year turnover. Inter-state services don't force early registration (Notification 10/2017-IT). No GST charged, no monthly returns; register within 30 days of crossing; QRMP = quarterly filings under ₹5 crore.
    Biggest residual risks?Viewer monetization creep, undisclosed paid rankings, prize-competition licensing (16 states), Ponzi law if you take deposits/referral chains, minors + DPDP, gig-work recharacterization.
