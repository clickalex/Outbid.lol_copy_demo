# 09 · Guardrails — the list of things that end Level 0

Read this once now, and again every time someone (including you, on a good day) says *"just add…"*. Every item
below is one small feature that silently converts a plain advertising business into a regulated one. At Level 0
each of them costs you either a compliance department you can't staff or a banking relationship you can't get back.

---

## 1. Never, at Level 0 (each one changes what you legally *are*)

| Don't | Because |
| --- | --- |
| Let a **visitor pay you anything** — entry fee, "boost", "retry", "priority review", coins, tips | Visitors who pay to play = stakes. Stakes = online money gaming: Gaming (Regulation) Act framework, 28%-on-full-value GST, and every bank and processor's prohibited list. One ₹10 button does this. |
| Let a visitor **earn** anything: cash, coupons, points, "watch to earn", referral income | Prize-winner duties: s.194B TDS with PAN capture, Form 26Q/16A, monthly deposits, payout rails with penny-drop KYC, state prize-law analysis, gig-work recharacterisation. It's a payroll system wearing a growth-hack costume. (This is **Level 1/2**, and `01`–`05` all get rewritten.) |
| Any **balance, wallet or credit** on the site, even "store credit" | Money you owe on demand = payments infrastructure you are not licensed to run. Keep it as "we refunded you" and nothing else. |
| **Self-issued tokens/coins/points redeemable in anything** | Money Circulation Act exposure + a securities-shaped object you now have to explain to a regulator for the fun of a leaderboard. |
| Let users **stake/lock** earned rewards "to grow them" | Betting. Full stop. |
| **Self-bidding your own board** to make it look busy, or buying a row through a friend to set a price floor | This is the deception that turns a legitimate product into misleading advertisement under the Consumer Protection Act, and it is the pattern refund teams and chargeback desks look for first. If you want your own project on the board, list it and **label it as yours**. |
| **Crypto / stablecoin rails**, "Monero for privacy", gift-card payments, cash-in-courier, third-party payers | Two taxable events instead of one (revenue at receipt **plus** 30% + cess on transfer of the VDA, no loss set-off), s.194S TDS tracking, Schedule VDA, VDA-service-provider arguments under PMLA, an AML surface you can't staff, and near-zero revenue in this market — 14 crypto-rail boards in your own inventory total **$154** lifetime. Not a tax shelter; a tax complication. |
| Publish **traffic/revenue/earnings promises** ("₹/day", "10k visitors guaranteed", "rank #1 on Google") | A promise you can't control is a misleading advertisement, and it manufactures refund claims by design. Sell the placement; say what you can't do (`05` Part C). |
| Open **comments, profiles, uploads** "just for feedback" | Intermediary duties (IT Rules grievance machinery), Copyright Rules takedown process, moderating a room you can't afford to moderate. You publish every row yourself — keep being the publisher. |
| Add an **age-restricted rewards gate** to "fix" the above | The gate only makes sense once rewards exist. Don't build the ladder before the wall. |
| Accept money from anyone you couldn't name in a regulator's file | Sanctions and AML: you may be too small to be regulated, not too small to be *asked*. Refuse opaque payers. |

## 2. "Later, with the right paperwork" (the ladder, in order — one rung per launch, not three)

1. **Level 0** (now): creators pay; nobody earns; the pack in this folder is the whole compliance surface.
2. **Level 1:** capped third-party coupons/credits, **under ₹10,000 per user per FY** so no TDS threshold is ever
   crossed, 18+, no cash equivalent, no resale. Adds: reward T&C (eligibility, measurement, caps, clawback, fraud),
   a voucher-purchase process, a PAN-capture trigger as a user nears the cap, and one counsel read of the design.
3. **Level 2:** cash payouts. Adds: payout provider + penny-drop, the 194B engine, monthly TDS deposits, 26Q/16A
   calendar, AML-lite screening at payout, a formal grievance officer, and a gaming-law opinion. Realistic lead
   time: 4–6 weeks plus ₹25–75k of counsel.
4. **Level 3+** (only if the board is bigger than you): accounts, admin staff, entity + GST + LUT for foreign
   revenue, contracts that buyers demand, insurance, and a second person who can publish when you're ill.

**One-question rule before building anything from list 2:** *"which document in this pack has to change first,
and who signs it?"* If you can't answer, you haven't designed a feature, you've designed an obligation.

## 3. The five lines to keep in the product, permanently (they're already written into `01`)

1. **"We sell a placement, not an outcome."** (Terms 1.2)
2. **"Sorted by amount paid, highest first. Not by merit."** (Terms 3.1 + the disclosure block)
3. **"Every row is labelled paid."** (05)
4. **"Visitors never pay and never earn here."** (Terms 1.3 — the sentence that keeps you out of gaming law)
5. **"We may stop; you get 30 days' notice and any undelivered period back."** (Terms 10.1 — the sentence that
   lets you walk away clean)

If any of those five ever feels like an obstacle to conversion, it is working. That is the entire Level 0
compact: **a smaller business you can describe to a bank in one paragraph, versus a bigger one you'd have to
explain to a regulator.**
