What the ladder says

Directories of bid sites post the highest median in the market at $63.00 — 3.1× the market median — and 42.9% of their measurable boards clear $100. The pick-and-shovel play beat the gold rush, as it usually does.

Regional & other currencies come second at $33.63, with a 25.0% hit rate over $100. A local audience and a local payment rail beat a global audience with neither.

Games & clips is the trap: 23 listings between them, $281 combined, and not one board over $100. High enthusiasm, no willingness to pay.

The category you probably want is the worst one

249 of 461 clones — 54.0% — chose Websites & products, the exact shape of the original. They compete with each other for the same buyers, and their median board takes $23.60.

Meanwhile the 23 measurable boards in the two best-performing categories took $6,692 between them — against $12,066 for the entire 294-board tail. Scarcity of competitors, not size of audience, is what pays here.

Three honest paths

If the simulator returned a bad verdict, these are the three responses the evidence actually supports.

Recommended

Don't build a board. Build the picks.

Tooling for the boards — directories, verification, comparison, analytics — posts the best median in the market and the highest hit rate, from the smallest number of competitors.

  • Best category today: Directories of bid sites
  • 13 listed · 7 measurable · $2,499 combined
  • Median $63.00, hit rate 42.9% over $100
  • Your customers are the operators, not a cold audience
  • No auction mechanic to defend, moderate or refund
Conditional

Go narrow, local, and priced properly.

The second-best category is a regional one, and the best-performing pricing tier is the most expensive. Both point the same way: fewer, better-qualified bidders beat volume.

  • Runner-up category: Regional & other currencies at $33.63
  • Boards priced above $25 median $602.00
  • Boards priced under $5 median $15.50
  • One city, one profession, one language, one local rail
  • Only worth it if you already reach that audience
Default

Bid on a board instead of building one.

The median board takes $20.00 across its entire life. A bid of that size on an established board buys the same exposure for one evening less work and no ongoing liability.

  • Median board attracts 2 bidders in total
  • 52.3% of boards have never been clicked
  • Click data is public before you spend anything
  • No domain, hosting, moderation or refund exposure
  • Reversible: stop after a single round

Assumptions

What this model does, what it deliberately does not do, and where it will mislead you.

The sample

323 measurable clone boards from the committed inventory snapshot — every board in the CC BY 4.0 outoutbid.lol directory with a readable public total, excluding outbid.lol itself so the model describes the market a new entrant joins. 138 boards publish no total at all; they are excluded, never counted as zero.

The maths

Hit rates are empirical frequencies: the share of boards in your category whose measured total met your target. No curve is fitted and no growth is projected. Fee drag uses a 2.9% + $0.30 card model, the common rail among the boards that name a processor.

Survivorship works against you

Boards abandoned before the directory could read them are missing from the sample, and 73 more were checked but unreadable. Both omissions remove failures rather than successes, so the real odds are very likely worse than the ones shown here.

What it cannot tell you

Totals are self-reported by each board and unaudited. Lifetime take is not profit — it excludes fees, hosting, chargebacks and your own time. A category median is not a promise: the same category can contain the market's biggest board and a dozen zeros.
